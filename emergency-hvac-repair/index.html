What Needs an Urgent HVAC Call

Urgent problems include a complete loss of cooling during extreme heat, no heat during a cold snap, water leaking from an air handler or ceiling, repeated breaker trips, sparking, or an electrical burning smell. Homes with infants, older adults, or medically vulnerable residents may need faster action when indoor temperatures become unsafe.

If you can do so safely, turn the HVAC system off when you see water near electrical components, smoke, sparking, or severe overheating. Do not open electrical panels or keep resetting a breaker that trips repeatedly.

Gas or Carbon Monoxide Safety

If you smell gas, hear a gas leak, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home immediately. From a safe location, call 911 or your gas utility emergency line. Do not operate switches, use a flame, or remain inside waiting for an HVAC technician.

What to Tell the Dispatcher

Describe whether the system is completely off, running without cooling or heating, leaking water, making unusual noises, producing an odor, or tripping a breaker. Also mention any thermostat message and whether the equipment is a central AC system, heat pump, gas furnace, or electric air handler if you know.

A breaker that trips again may be protecting the system from an electrical fault. Do not keep resetting it. Leave the equipment off and call for diagnosis.
